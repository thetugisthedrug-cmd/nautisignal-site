Offshore / Canyons

The five-day canyon marlin tournament

300 BOATS5 DAYS70+ NM OFFSHORESEVEN-FIGURE PURSEFULL SIGNAL + SATELLITE

The setup

A canyon fleet running seventy-plus miles to the edge — off the grid from the first hour to the last, with a purse that turns every scoring question into a legal question. Radios arrive provisioned and paired to the boat list, with satellite backup for the canyons; the boundary, daily fishing hours, and confirmation codes are configured and checked off before day one. The weigh-in stage TV feed and the live spectator page are switched on for the biggest crowd of the summer.

How the week runs

Lines-in and lines-out run on the schedule automation every day — the clock is the server's, not anyone's wristwatch. A white marlin release is called on the channel and the whole fleet hears it seventy miles apart; the report lands with the boat's identity, GPS fix, and server timestamp, and “Confirmed — Blue 113” goes back out fleet-wide while the code posts to the boat's handheld with a logged acknowledgment. Five days of releases build one leaderboard, live at the marina and on every phone at home. And the mayday chain matters most out here: one orange button interrupts the entire fleet, positions the casualty, and asks the nearest boats by name — when help is seventy miles from shore, help is each other.

The difference

With that much money on the line, the record is the product. Every report of the week carries its recording, timestamp, position, code, and acknowledgment — so a seven-figure protest is a five-minute lookup, not a week in arbitration. Sponsors ride the weigh-in feed, the live page, and every handheld in the fleet, all week long.

Sailfish / Near-shore

The winter sailfish bite off Miami

75 BOATS3 DAYS2–5 MI OFFSHOREPRO SIGNAL

The setup

A kite fleet working the color change just a few miles off the beach — close enough that the crowd can almost watch from shore, and close enough that cell coverage is strong all day. No radios required: the captain app carries the whole event on Pro Signal, and the release format means everything is scored by call-in, GPS, and timestamp.

How the day runs

A front pushes through and the bite goes off — eleven releases in twenty minutes across the fleet. Every report lands with its GPS fix and server timestamp attached; clean releases confirm automatically while the director reviews only the ones that need a decision, and each confirmation code is announced fleet-wide as it posts. The leaderboard swings in real time on the dock and on every phone following from home.

The difference

The flurry is exactly what breaks a radio-and-clipboard operation, and exactly what this one absorbs: no backlog, no lost release, no double-counted double-header. When two boats release within the same minute on the final day, the tie sorts by server seconds — not by whose recollection of the radio traffic wins the argument.

Kingfish / Jackpot

The 120-boat kingfish jackpot

120 BOATS1 DAYCASH JACKPOTPRO SIGNAL

The setup

Real money on the line and a huge, mixed field — weekend crews next to full-time charters. App and text reporting fit every boat without new hardware, and lines-in / lines-out run on the schedule automation so the clock is never a judgment call.

How the day runs

Catches flow in by app with GPS attached; the leaderboard moves live on the dock TVs and in every family's pocket. Text alerts keep captains current. When the last-hour flurry hits, every report carries a server timestamp — the 3:00:00 cutoff is exact, and a 3:00:41 report is flagged automatically instead of argued about.

The difference

Scoring closes with a one-click export in receive order with the official tie-break sequence and a single counts-for-scoring column. The jackpot pays out the same evening, with a record nobody can dispute.

Inshore / Redfish

The inshore redfish classic

60 SKIFFS1 DAYBAY BOUNDARYPRO SIGNAL

The setup

A catch-photo-release format in shallow water where the boundary is the whole game — creek mouths, closed zones, a line you cannot see from a poling platform. The director draws the boundary once in the dashboard, and every report is checked against it.

How the day runs

App reports carry the GPS fix with them. A fish reported outside the line is flagged the moment it arrives — a review item at 10 AM, not a rumor at weigh-in. Clean reports confirm automatically, so the director reviews only the handful that need a human decision. Families follow the whole day from the ramp on the live page.

The difference

Boundary disputes go from the ugliest part of the day to a non-event: the fix, the timestamp, and the flag are logged before the boat is back on plane. The director spends the day running the event, not refereeing it.

Bass / Series

The lakeside bass series

90 BOATSSEASON SERIESFOG DELAYBASE → PRO

The setup

A club series on a budget: call-in and text scoring covers the regular season, with the live broadcast and leaderboard added for championship day. No new habits — captains report the way they always have, and NautiSignal captures and logs every one.

How the day runs

A fog delay on championship morning takes one spoken announcement to reach every boat at once, with text backup to every registered captain — no phone tree, no boat left waiting at the wrong ramp. Staggered lines-in run on the schedule, and standings update as reports land.

The difference

The series upgrades for its biggest day without changing how a single angler reports. Time-stamped records across the season make year-end standings a report, not a recount.

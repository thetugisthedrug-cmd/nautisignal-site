Case Studies

Any fishing tournament. Any water.

Four model events showing how NautiSignal runs a day on the water — offshore, jackpot, inshore, and series. Every capability shown here ships today.

These are illustrative event profiles, not client endorsements — each one is drawn directly from how the platform runs a real tournament day.

Offshore / Billfish

The two-day billfish invitational

85 BOATS2 DAYS60 NM OFFSHOREFULL SIGNAL

The setup

Big sportfishers running far past cell coverage, releases scored by call-in, and protests historically settled by memory and a handwritten radio log. Radios arrive provisioned and paired to the boat list; the boundary, fishing hours, and confirmation codes are configured and checked off before day one. The marina TV feed and the live spectator page are switched on.

How the day runs

A release is called on the tournament channel and the whole fleet hears it as it happens. The report is captured with the boat's identity, GPS fix, and a server timestamp; the director confirms, and “Confirmed — Blue 113” is spoken fleet-wide while the code lands on the reporting boat's handheld with a logged acknowledgment. The mayday chain stays armed all day: one orange button interrupts the fleet, positions the casualty, and hands the director a ready-to-read script for the authorities.

The difference

A weigh-in protest that once meant an hour of arguing is settled in minutes: the original voice recording, the timestamp, the position, the code, and the boat's own acknowledgment are all on one screen. Sponsors ride the TV feed and every handheld between the action.

Kingfish / Jackpot

The 120-boat kingfish jackpot

120 BOATS1 DAYCASH JACKPOTPRO SIGNAL

The setup

Real money on the line and a huge, mixed field — weekend crews next to full-time charters. App and text reporting fit every boat without new hardware, and lines-in / lines-out run on the schedule automation so the clock is never a judgment call.

How the day runs

Catches flow in by app with GPS attached; the leaderboard moves live on the dock TVs and in every family's pocket. Text alerts keep captains current. When the last-hour flurry hits, every report carries a server timestamp — the 3:00:00 cutoff is exact, and a 3:00:41 report is flagged automatically instead of argued about.

The difference

Scoring closes with a one-click export in receive order with the official tie-break sequence and a single counts-for-scoring column. The jackpot pays out the same evening, with a record nobody can dispute.

Inshore / Redfish

The inshore redfish classic

60 SKIFFS1 DAYBAY BOUNDARYPRO SIGNAL

The setup

A catch-photo-release format in shallow water where the boundary is the whole game — creek mouths, closed zones, a line you cannot see from a poling platform. The director draws the boundary once in the dashboard, and every report is checked against it.

How the day runs

App reports carry the GPS fix with them. A fish reported outside the line is flagged the moment it arrives — a review item at 10 AM, not a rumor at weigh-in. Clean reports confirm automatically, so the director reviews only the handful that need a human decision. Families follow the whole day from the ramp on the live page.

The difference

Boundary disputes go from the ugliest part of the day to a non-event: the fix, the timestamp, and the flag are logged before the boat is back on plane. The director spends the day running the event, not refereeing it.

Bass / Series

The lakeside bass series

90 BOATSSEASON SERIESFOG DELAYBASE → PRO

The setup

A club series on a budget: call-in and text scoring covers the regular season, with the live broadcast and leaderboard added for championship day. No new habits — captains report the way they always have, and NautiSignal captures and logs every one.

How the day runs

A fog delay on championship morning takes one spoken announcement to reach every boat at once, with text backup to every registered captain — no phone tree, no boat left waiting at the wrong ramp. Staggered lines-in run on the schedule, and standings update as reports land.

The difference

The series upgrades for its biggest day without changing how a single angler reports. Time-stamped records across the season make year-end standings a report, not a recount.
